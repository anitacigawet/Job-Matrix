# Monthly Retrospective — design notes

A long-form, narrative report. The listener reads this once a month and gets a story of their search — not a list of stats, but an actual retrospective written like a thoughtful friend who's been watching closely.

## Instructions (sent to Studio)

Produce a **markdown document** of 1,200–1,800 words structured as a monthly retrospective on the listener's job search. The voice is reflective, warm, observation-rich — closer to a personal essay than a corporate report.

**Structure.**

### Opening

One paragraph framing the month. What month is this, in their search? (First month? Sixth?) What was the headline trajectory — momentum building, stalled, pivoting? Don't make it up; ground it in the data.

### The numbers, in plain English

A short section translating the raw counts into a coherent story. Not a stat dump. ("Across the month: 240 listings scanned, 60 cleared the AI filter, 18 scored above 80% fit, 9 applied, 4 responded, 2 reached interview, 1 ghost." Then a sentence interpreting that — where the funnel is healthy, where it's leaking.)

### Patterns worth naming

Three or four observations from the month, each with a paragraph of context:

- **Pattern 1.** Something the data revealed about the listener's targeting, behaviour, or the market. Specific. Grounded.
- **Pattern 2.** Same.
- **Pattern 3.** Same.
- (Pattern 4 if there's something else genuinely worth flagging.)

These aren't generic ("you should apply more!"); they're observations only someone watching closely could make ("most of your high-fit matches came from listings posted on Mondays — the platform's posting cadence might be worth optimising your scan schedule against").

### What's working

A short list (3–5 items) of things the listener should keep doing. Specific. Not "keep being positive" — actual practices the data suggests are paying off.

### What's not working

A parallel list of things to reconsider. Direct, kind. "You've been applying mostly to mid-level roles, but your strongest fit scores are at senior — worth shifting the targeting upward." Avoid the "constructive sandwich"; just say it.

### One experiment to run next month

A single concrete experiment to run in the coming month. Specific, measurable, scoped to four weeks. ("Set the auto-scan to fire Sunday evenings and target only Staff+ roles for two weeks; compare new-match volume.")

### Closing

A short, sincere closing paragraph. Acknowledge the work. No motivational poster vocabulary. End on a note of clarity, not hype.

**Tone constraints.**

- **Narrative, not bullet-list.** Most sections are paragraphs. The lists of working / not-working items are the exceptions.
- **Specific to this listener, this month.** A retrospective written for "the average job seeker" is useless. The whole point is observation.
- **Honest about emotional weight.** Job searching is hard. Acknowledge that where it shows in the data (long stretches of no responses, repeated rejections) without dwelling on it.

**Forbidden.**

- "You've got this!" "Keep going!" "Trust the process!" Any motivational-poster phrase.
- Inventing data points to make the narrative neater.
- Comparing the listener to "other job seekers" or generic benchmarks. The story is theirs.
- Generic career advice. The listener has search-specific tools for that.
