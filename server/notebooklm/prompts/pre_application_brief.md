# Pre-Application Brief — design notes

The listener is about to apply to a specific job. Before they hit submit, they want a short, well-organised markdown briefing that helps them tailor the application and approach it intelligently. Aim for the kind of doc you'd hope a thoughtful friend would write for you.

## Instructions (sent to Studio)

Produce a **markdown document** of approximately 600–900 words structured as a pre-application briefing for the specific role and company described in the source. The listener's profile is in the same source document — cross-reference it.

**Structure (use these section headings exactly).**

### 1. The Role at a Glance

A four-to-six bullet snapshot:
- Title and seniority signal.
- Company, stage / size if known from the source.
- Location / remote situation.
- Posted salary range, if any.
- The one or two most defining requirements (read between the lines of the job description).

### 2. What They're Actually Hiring For

Three to five sentences. Strip the boilerplate. What is the team actually trying to solve by hiring this person? What does the day-one-to-day-thirty look like? Cite specific phrases from the job description.

### 3. The Listener's Fit Angle

A short paragraph followed by three or four bullets. The paragraph names the headline fit — "Strong fit on X and Y; lighter on Z." The bullets break down exactly what to emphasise from the listener's profile to align with the role's requirements. Quote both sides — what the JD asks for, what the listener has.

If the fit is weak, say so plainly. Don't fabricate alignment.

### 4. Gaps, and How to Frame Them

For each non-trivial gap, one sentence on what the gap is and one sentence on the most honest framing the listener can use. ("They mention experience with X; the listener hasn't shipped X but has shipped Y, which exercises the same underlying skill. Frame it as 'I've solved that problem in a slightly different stack — here's how the transfer maps.'")

### 5. Résumé Tweaks for This Submission

Three to five concrete edits to the listener's résumé specifically for this submission. Each edit references the actual résumé content (if it's in the source data) and what to change. If the listener hasn't saved their résumé text, note that this section would be richer with it.

### 6. Cover Letter Seed

A short, plain-prose paragraph (not a whole letter) that the listener can use as the seed of a cover-letter opener. Specific to this role, this company. Not a template — actual sentences they could literally use.

### 7. Risk Flags

Anything in the job description that warrants caution before applying: red-flag language ("rockstar", "wear many hats", "fast-paced", "family"), salary far below the listener's stated minimum, location restrictions that conflict with the listener's preferences, scope creep signals. One or two sentences each. If there are no red flags, say so and move on.

**Tone constraints.**

- Direct, specific, well-organised. This is a working document, not a polished essay.
- Treat the listener as a competent adult. Skip basic application advice.
- Quote both the job description and the listener's profile freely — that's what makes the briefing useful.

**Forbidden.**

- Generic application advice ("tailor your résumé!", "research the company!"). The listener has Job Matrix for that.
- Fabricated company facts (funding, headcount, strategic direction) not present in the source.
- Hype framing of the role ("this is an amazing opportunity!"). Just describe it accurately.
- Personal opinions about whether the listener "should" apply. Surface the data; let them decide.
